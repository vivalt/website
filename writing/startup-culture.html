Arrogance, Brittleness, and Start-up Culture

Some thoughts on what happens when organizations grow around confidence and speed without building habits of reflection, dissent, and intellectual humility.

The Pattern

I appreciate entrepreneurial ventures, but there's a recurring pattern of arrogance among leaders, particularly in tech and politics, that concerns me. The origins might be inherent to ambitious founders, developed through success attribution, or fostered by surrounding yes-men. But arrogance leads to brittleness—it undermines the ability to update beliefs based on evidence or account for risks adequately.

Moving Fast vs. Moving Wisely

Startup culture celebrates "moving fast and breaking things," and there's appeal in that. But this mindset becomes dangerous without constraints. Efficiency matters, but unchecked leadership divorced from feedback mechanisms creates systemic fragility.

The Value of Constraints

Democratic norms, rules, and constraints—while sometimes inefficient—serve protective functions. The mentality that "constraints hold back strong leaders" ignores that humans are prone to overshoot and miss the mark without structural safeguards.

Some constraints are good, actually, given humans' predispositions. Not too many, but some.

Rather than eliminating all constraints, we need balance. The question isn't whether to have constraints, but which ones serve us well.

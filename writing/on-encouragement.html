On Encouragement

A short piece on how much encouragement matters in academic life, especially when ability is hard to distinguish from confidence and persistence.

The Problem with Encouragement

People should pay more attention to who is giving the advice, how it benefits them, and whether advisors truly understand the actual odds of success.

Misaligned Incentives

PhD advisors may encourage students to pursue academic positions with poor success rates. The advisor benefits if the student succeeds but faces minimal consequences if they fail, creating self-interested bias toward optimism about outcomes.

The Advice-Giver's Dilemma

Those offering counsel face pressure to appear supportive. Negative assessments risk being rejected and damaging relationships, so advisers shade their assessments up unconsciously, even without deliberate deception.

Institutional Pressures

Competition structures sometimes incentivize optimism—institutions want many quality applications despite low success odds, fostering systematically inflated expectations.

A Warning

Scrutinize encouragement sources and recognize that optimism can be helpful at the societal level while remaining personally protective of your own risk exposure. Question who benefits from your risk-taking and consider downside consequences carefully.

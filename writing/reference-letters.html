On Reference Letters (for Undergraduates)

Advice for students on when to ask for letters, how to ask, and what kinds of materials make it easier for faculty to write a useful one.

How Reference Letters Are Evaluated

When students list references, programs typically request comparative ratings across dimensions like work ethic and integrity, using scales such as "top 1%" through "below average." These structured responses significantly influence screening decisions.

Consider the other side of the market—understand the referee's perspective and what makes their job easier while strengthening your application.

My Policy on Reference Requests

I decline to write letters for students performing below average in my courses unless exceptional circumstances exist that would justify a high rating. My reasoning: maintaining credibility in future recommendations requires consistency between performance and endorsements.

What Makes Strong Letters

Generic letters reflecting only transcript information provide minimal additional value. Outstanding recommendations include specific examples demonstrating curiosity, engagement, analytical thinking, or research contributions that admissions committees cannot discern from applications alone.

Practical Recommendations

  • Request references from courses where you excelled
  • Contact professors at least three weeks before deadlines
  • Provide a list of target programs and their submission dates
  • Share information about program types you're targeting upfront

These steps help professors craft more substantive letters while managing their time effectively.
